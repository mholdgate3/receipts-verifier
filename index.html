Receipts verifier

Checks a receipt without trusting Receipts: it re-hashes the claim record, folds its Merkle proof, and reads the anchor transaction from the chain itself. Give it the post's text or photos from any copy and it checks that the record is about those words and those photos. Give it an archived copy of the post's page and it checks who wrote it. Give it a settlement and it works out the outcome and the baseline again from the settlement's own data. Give it a caller's checkpoint and it says how recent it is: given every record up to it, none was held back. Give it a caller's whole export and it works their score out again from the records themselves. Nothing here calls our servers.

Links, line breaks and leading @replies are ignored, so copy only the post's own words; the result shows exactly what was compared. Emoji count, so a copy that drops them, as some do where a page shows emoji as pictures, won't match.

Only the exact files X serves match: a screenshot, crop or resized copy never will. The result says where to get each one. The files stay on your computer.

The result gives the raw address of each copy the receipt lists: save that page, or the archive's copy of X's answer for the post, and give it here. It is read as text to find who wrote the post and what it said, never opened as a page, and stays on your computer.

A file the settlement cites is checked against the SHA-256 it cites, which is the one Binance publishes beside the file. Where it left Binance out, any other file is read as Binance's data, to see whether Binance would have agreed; check each of those against Binance's checksum yourself. The result lists the files and their checksums.
